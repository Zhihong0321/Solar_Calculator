# Why Eternalgy’s Solar Simulation Is Designed to Be Closer to Your Actual TNB Savings

## A solar investment should be measured against the bill you will actually pay

A solar proposal can look impressive when the saving is based on a simple percentage:

> “Your bill is RM600. Solar can save 80%. Your saving is RM480 per month.”

That is not a bill simulation. It is an assumption.

Eternalgy takes a more rigorous approach. We begin with the customer’s actual TNB bill, translate that bill into its corresponding electricity usage and tariff structure, simulate how solar energy moves through the property, and then calculate the projected TNB bill again after solar.

The result is not merely a “saving percentage.” It is a before-and-after bill comparison built from the way the customer actually uses and pays for electricity.

> **We do not start by asking how much saving would look attractive. We start with the customer’s real bill and calculate what should remain after solar.**

---

## Why many solar-saving estimates miss the real outcome

A shortcut estimate usually treats every ringgit on a TNB bill as if it can be removed at the same rate. In reality, the bill is affected by more than total solar generation.

The actual outcome depends on factors such as:

- the electricity usage represented by the customer’s bill;
- the applicable TNB tariff components;
- how much solar the proposed system can generate;
- how much electricity is consumed while solar is being produced;
- how much energy is stored and later discharged by a battery;
- battery usable capacity and energy losses;
- how much energy is still imported from the grid;
- how much excess solar is exported and the value of that export;
- EEI, AFA, SST and bill-cycle treatment; and
- the actual system price used to calculate payback.

Ignoring these interactions may produce a beautiful quotation, but it can also produce the wrong investment expectation.

## How Eternalgy’s simulation works

### 1. We anchor the calculation to the customer’s actual TNB bill

The customer’s bill amount is matched against the TNB tariff data for the selected historical AFA period. This reverse lookup estimates the monthly electricity usage in kWh represented by that bill and reconstructs the bill components.

This matters because two customers with the same solar system can receive very different savings if their consumption and tariff positions are different.

### 2. We size the solar system from electricity usage

The calculator uses the estimated monthly kWh, selected sun-peak-hour assumption, panel wattage and panel quantity to determine the proposed system size and monthly solar generation.

This connects the recommended system to the customer’s energy requirement instead of selecting a package only because it fits a sales target.

### 3. We simulate where every part of the solar energy goes

Solar energy does not all have the same financial value. The simulation separates it into practical energy flows:

- solar used directly by the property;
- solar sent into the battery;
- energy delivered by the battery after losses;
- excess solar exported to the grid;
- energy beyond the usable offset or export path; and
- electricity that must still be imported from TNB.

Direct self-consumption, battery discharge and export are therefore not treated as if they were identical.

### 4. We include battery limits and losses

A battery cannot create energy, store unlimited energy or discharge its full nameplate capacity every day. Eternalgy’s calculation accounts for:

- battery capacity;
- depth of discharge;
- charging and discharging losses;
- available excess solar; and
- the property’s remaining night-time demand.

The simulated battery contribution is restricted by the smallest real constraint—not by the largest marketing number.

### 5. We calculate the post-solar TNB bill again

After solar self-consumption and battery contribution are applied, the calculator determines the remaining grid usage and performs another tariff lookup.

It then rebuilds the projected post-solar bill, including the relevant usage, network, capacity, SST, EEI and AFA treatment. Export value is calculated separately and applied to the payable outcome.

This is the key difference:

> **A percentage estimate subtracts an assumption from the old bill. Eternalgy simulates the new bill.**

### 6. We show the comparison customers actually care about

The customer can see:

- the original TNB bill represented by the matched tariff data;
- the projected TNB bill after solar;
- projected monthly saving;
- bill reduction, EEI saving and export value;
- solar generation, self-use, battery contribution, export and grid import;
- final system cost; and
- estimated payback period.

This makes the proposal easier to question, verify and explain.

The honest comparison is not “simulation versus no calculation.” It is **a bill-based energy-flow simulation versus a broad percentage assumption**.

## Why a small monthly error can become a 20-year investment problem

Solar is a long-term asset. That means an inaccurate monthly expectation does not remain a small monthly mistake—it repeats for years.

### Illustrative example

Assume a system costs **RM45,000**.

| | Saving presented | Saving actually achieved |
|---|---:|---:|
| Monthly saving | RM600 | RM450 |
| First-year saving | RM7,200 | RM5,400 |
| Simple payback | 6.3 years | 8.3 years |
| 20-year saving, without escalation | RM144,000 | RM108,000 |
| 20-year expectation gap |  | **RM36,000** |

If both figures are projected with a 3% annual tariff escalation, the cumulative gap grows to approximately **RM48,000** over 20 years.

A RM150 monthly overstatement can therefore:

- delay payback by about two years;
- reduce the expected long-term return by tens of thousands of ringgit;
- make financing payments feel very different from what was promised;
- turn a comfortable cash-flow plan into a monthly burden; and
- damage the customer’s trust long after installation.

This is why a solar proposal should not be judged only by how high the saving looks. It should be judged by how the saving was calculated.

> **The most expensive solar quotation is not always the one with the highest price. It may be the one built on the least accurate expectation.**

## What “precise” means—and what it does not mean

Eternalgy’s simulation is designed to be more precise because it models more of the variables that determine the customer’s actual bill. Precision describes the quality and transparency of the method; it is not a guarantee that every future bill will be identical to the projection.

Actual results can still change because of:

- weather and seasonal solar conditions;
- roof direction, tilt, shading, dirt and site-specific system losses;
- changes in household or business consumption;
- equipment availability and maintenance;
- future TNB tariffs, AFA, taxes and export rules;
- battery operating behaviour;
- billing-period length; and
- long-term equipment degradation.

For the strongest assessment, Eternalgy should use a representative bill history—preferably up to 12 months—together with an accurate site survey and realistic consumption profile.

The current long-term savings chart should be presented as a **forecast scenario**, not a guaranteed return. It extends the simulated monthly saving and assumes 3% annual tariff escalation. Future marketing should not imply that weather variation, tariff changes or panel degradation are known with certainty.
